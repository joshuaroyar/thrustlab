task 1 : (light blue color : front part)
    Fan rotor: The fan rotor is the first rotor in the compressor. Its primary task is to accelerate a large mass flow of air in the bypass flow and thus provide the main thrust.

taks 2 : (metalllic grey)
    Gearbox: In one of the most advanced turbofan engines, the geared turbofan, the gearbox decouples the low-pressure turbine and the fan. In conventional engines, these two components sit on one shaft.

task 3: (green color )
    Low-pressure compressor: The task of the compressors is to ingest air and compress it before it is fed into the combustor. The low-pressure compressor is responsible for precompressing the air.

task 4: ( yellow color)
    High-pressure compressor: The high-pressure compressor handles main compression. What sets an advanced turbofan engine apart is that the blades and disk are manufactured as a single part—known as a “blisk.”

task 5: (orange color)
    High-pressure turbine: In a turbine, the energy contained in the gas flow emerging at high pressure and temperature from the combustor is converted into mechanical energy. Comprising multiple blades, the high-pressure turbine drives the high-pressure compressor.

task 6 : (grey color in the middle back part of the engine)
    Combustor: Inside the combustor, the compressed air flowing into the chamber is mixed with fuel, where it burns at a temperature of about 1,700 degrees Celsius.

task 7: (dark blue)
    Low-pressure turbine: The low-pressure turbine drives the low-pressure compressor and the fan, which in turn generates the bulk of the thrust. The GTF’s low-pressure turbine rotates much faster than that of a conventional turbofan engine.
